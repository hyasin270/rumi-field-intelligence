A quiz from the lesson just taught

A teacher forwards one link to the class group, and the children take a quiz written from the lesson that teacher just taught. Until 6 October the quiz ran inside the WhatsApp chat, one message per step, about 46 for each finished quiz; on 1 October Meta began charging for every one of them. Since 7 October the same link opens a web page. Nothing is sent per question, and the page does what the chat could not: a voice for every question in English or Urdu, class and school leagues, a friend challenge, a video library and short reading and number games.

Why it moved

The quiz first ran inside the WhatsApp chat. A question was a message, every answer was a reply, and the feedback, the scorecard and the offers after it were more messages: about 46 for one finished quiz. Through September that cost nothing: replies a business sends inside a conversation the person has started were free.

On 1 October that changed. Meta now gives each number 1,000 free replies a month and charges for the rest; in Pakistan it is US$0.015 a message. A number like NIETE’s uses the free thousand within the first day of the month. At that rate the chat quiz costs about 67 cents for every finished quiz.

A quiz does not need the chat: the same link opens a web page in one tap. So the link still travels the way it always did, from teacher to class group to a family phone, and everything after the tap happens in a page that sends no messages. All in, a finished quiz on the page costs two to four cents; the recorded voices and the one message that carries the link are nine-tenths of that.

Saving the messages was the reason. What the page opened up is the rest of this story.

Jugnu’s Challenge

Two more came later: Missing number (find the number missing from a row) and, for grade 1, Numbers (count the dots, pick the number). Do children come back on their own?

A new class link from the teacher brings children to the quiz. The question here is what happens without one. A child counts as coming back on their own on a day when they start a quiz, have played on an earlier day, and nothing new reached them: no class link made that day or the day before for their class, or for any other child who plays on the same phone. We count those children each school day, and set them against the children who could have come back: everyone who played in the 14 days before. A school day is one on which teachers got at least 30% of a usual weekday’s class links, so Sundays and holidays drop out.

Has anything changed it?

For each change, the share of the children who had played in the 14 days before is compared on the school days after it with the same weekdays in the four weeks before. Since the home-screen icon release on 22 Nov: too early to tell. We wait for five school days after it; so far there are two. Since reminders went on: too early to tell. We wait for five school days after it; so far there are two. Urban-II, where reminders started first, against the other sectors over the same days: too early to tell. We wait for five school days after it; so far there are two. Since the standing class link went on: too early to tell. We wait for five school days after it; so far there is one.

This is a before-and-after comparison: exams, holidays, the weather and other changes move the same line, so it cannot show that a change caused a rise or a fall. When reminders start in one sector first, comparing the children of that sector’s classes with the children of the other sectors’ classes over the same days is the fairer test; one sector is still one group, so we read it as strong only if the gap holds for two weeks. Children who share a phone are counted together for new links, which can only make the count smaller; a link a teacher forwards days after it was made looks old here, which can make it larger. A school day with no school day on the same weekday in the four weeks before (such as a Sunday that counted as a school day) is left out of the comparison. Quizzes started on our own testers’ phones, marked since 22 November, are taken out: none so far. Counts under 5 are not shown. How this is counted was written down and fixed before any number was computed, and each version kept: version 1 (sha256 13285692…), version 2 (077128b6…), the reminder test’s sector and groups (47724df6…).

How much weight this carries

  • The web page is 49 days old and changed during its first two (the list under “Since the page went live”). Jugnu’s Challenge, as described, has run for one evening and 48 full days.
  • Our own testing is taken out. Test children and their quiz codes are removed by record; hub and game taps, which carry no quiz code, are removed for the minutes our checks ran (7 Oct 12:20–13:10; 7 Oct 19:30–20:00; 7 Oct 21:45–22:15; 8 Oct 09:20–09:45).
  • A storybook is not a quiz. A storybook a teacher sends opens with no questions and cannot be finished, so its 3,376 sittings, and the 457 children who opened nothing else, are left out of every count of quizzes and children on this page.
  • A child on the page is a new record, so a child on both routes is counted twice and some children called new since 7 October had played on the chat before (see “New and returning”).
  • The children are self-selected. They are the ones whose families opened a link. Nobody was watching them answer.
  • Every name on this page is invented. The screens come from our test copy of the service; no child’s or teacher’s name, class or number appears here.
