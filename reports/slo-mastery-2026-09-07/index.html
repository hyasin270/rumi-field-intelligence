NIETE · Islamabad · two days

Did the children get what their teachers taught?

Every quiz here was written from a real lesson recording: the teacher taught, the system read the transcript back, listed the objectives the lesson actually covered, and asked the children about those objectives — not about a syllabus.

311 quizzes sent to teachers 131 reached a child 752 children answered 583 finished all eight questions 6–8 September 2026

77.9%

of the objectives children were asked about, they mastered — meaning they got every question on that objective right. Across 2719 child-and-objective pairs from 583 finished quizzes.

Question by question the figure is higher — 85.3% of individual questions were answered correctly, and the median child scored 88%. The stricter reading is the useful one: a child who misses one of the two questions on fractions as parts of a whole has not mastered it, even though their score still looks good.

Apply questions are the ones children lose

Every question is written at a level — remembering a fact, understanding an idea, or applying it to something new. Questions that ask a child to apply come back six points below the rest.

How much weight this carries

  • Two days, 583 children, 131 lessons. The headline has held between 77.9% and 78.8% as the sample grew from 103 children to 583 — that number is real. The cuts beneath it are less settled: the grade gap that looked convincing on day one has already washed out. Per-lesson figures still rest on a handful of children each; treat a single lesson's number as a conversation starter with that teacher, not a measurement.
  • The children are self-selected. They are the ones whose parents opened a WhatsApp link on the day. Nothing here says what the rest of the class knows.
  • Nobody was watching them answer. A quiz taken at home can be taken with help.
  • The objectives are the machine's reading of the lesson, taken from the transcript — an accurate summary of what was said, not a curriculum document the teacher signed off.
  • 85% of questions correct is a high ceiling. If it holds as numbers grow, the questions may be pitched slightly easy — that is a tuning question for the next week of data, and it would push the mastery figure down, not up.
