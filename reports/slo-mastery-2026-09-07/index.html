A quiz from the lesson just taught

A teacher forwards one link to the class group, and the children take a quiz written from the lesson that teacher just taught. Until 6 October the quiz ran inside the WhatsApp chat, one message per step, about 46 for each finished quiz; on 1 October Meta began charging for every one of them. Since 7 October the same link opens a web page. Nothing is sent per question, and the page does what the chat could not: a voice for every question in English or Urdu, class and school leagues, a friend challenge, a video library and short reading and number games.

Why it moved

The quiz first ran inside the WhatsApp chat. A question was a message, every answer was a reply, and the feedback, the scorecard and the offers after it were more messages: about 46 for one finished quiz. Through September that cost nothing: replies a business sends inside a conversation the person has started were free.

On 1 October that changed. Meta now gives each number 1,000 free replies a month and charges for the rest; in Pakistan it is US$0.015 a message. A number like NIETE’s uses the free thousand within the first day of the month. At that rate the chat quiz costs about 67 cents for every finished quiz.

A quiz does not need the chat: the same link opens a web page in one tap. So the link still travels the way it always did, from teacher to class group to a family phone, and everything after the tap happens in a page that sends no messages. All in, a finished quiz on the page costs two to four cents; the recorded voices and the one message that carries the link are nine-tenths of that.

Saving the messages was the reason. What the page opened up is the rest of this story.

Links sent before the switch still open the old way, and those chat sittings are dwindling as the old links age. Since 22:03 on 7 October an old link answers with one button that opens the quiz on the page; the button has been sent 281 times by 23:59. 2,083 of the web sittings were in English and 1,627 in Urdu. 627 of the 2,389 children were matched to their class’s list, so their results land against the right name in the teacher’s register.

Of the 3,722 page opens that carry a browser signature, 59 name WhatsApp’s own browser; the rest opened in the phone’s own browser (Chrome on most phones), which is where a link forwarded in a class group opens. So the quiz is one tap from WhatsApp, not inside it.

Jugnu’s Challenge, day one

Jugnu’s Challenge is three short practice games for grades 2 to 5, reached from the hub: Which is bigger? (ten pairs of numbers), Read aloud (a passage read for one minute, words lighting up as the child reads, then questions on it) and Listen and answer (a story read to the child, then questions). The games opened at 12:39 on 7 October; the version described here, with the live reading, the questions and the listening game, went on at 19:38 that evening. So “day one” here runs from 7 Oct 19:38 to 23:59 on 8 October (Pakistan time), and 8 October is its first full school day.

“Started” counts each time a game was opened, so a child who opens a game twice counts twice and the finished share is a floor. 7 Oct 20:00, 7 Oct 22:00, 7 Oct 23:00, 8 Oct 14:00, 8 Oct 15:00, 8 Oct 21:00 are the hours (Pakistan time) in which a game was finished. Where the drop happens: a child reaches the games through the scorecard’s door to the hub, then the games tile, and the games are for grades 2 to 5 only. In these hours the hub was opened 479 times, the games menu 67 times, and 41 games were started. A WCPM from the page is practice, not an assessment: nobody checks who is reading, and a child can read the same passage again.

  • A child is active in a week when they start a quiz. New means that week holds their first quiz ever, on any quiz since the service began; back means they had played before.

Among teachers, the share sending a quiz for the first time fell week by week, from 96% in the first week; returning teachers first outnumbered new ones in the week of 28 Sep – 4 Oct (56% returning), and 73% in 5–8 Oct (so far). In the web days, 78% of the 251 teachers whose class link went out had sent one before.

Children are where the switch blurs the count. A child on the chat is known by the family phone; a child on the web page is known by the page’s own record, made the first time they say who is playing. The two cannot yet be joined, so a child who played in September and now plays on the page counts as new. Of the 2,777 children since 7 October, 2,389 are page records and 100 of those played on the page on more than one day. A separate check that matched page children to chat children under the same teacher found 14–24% of page children had played on the chat before, so the true “new” share of children since 7 October is lower than the table shows. Until the two records can be joined, the weeks before 7 October are the clean read.

How far it has reached

Since the first quiz went out on 6 September, 11,801 children have started at least one quiz, from the classes of 1,796 teachers who got a class link for at least one quiz. Who is new and who came back is in “New and returning” above; this section is about where they are.

Where the children come from

Every teacher belongs to a school, and every ICT school to one of six sectors, so both the teachers and the children can be placed. Across the six, 24.5% of the 6,602 teachers on the roll have had a class link for at least one quiz, from 323 of the 433 schools. Sihala leads on penetration (32.7% of its teachers); Urban-I carries the most children (5,408) because it is the biggest sector. Mastery barely moves between them — Urban-I at 75.3%, Urban-II at 69.8% — which is the point: the quiz is written from the lesson, and the lessons are the same curriculum everywhere.

The same quiz again

3,208 children have finished a quiz and then sat that same quiz again — 27% of everyone reached, across 8,572 repeat sittings. It happens every day, weekends included. The class report and the class card count each child once, using their latest attempt, so a child who repeats a quiz to improve is ranked on the result they ended with.

How much weight this carries

  • The web page is two days old and changed during them (the list under “Since the page went live”). Jugnu’s Challenge, as described, is one evening and one school day. A day is not a week.
  • Our own testing is taken out. Test children and their quiz codes are removed by record; hub and game taps, which carry no quiz code, are removed for the minutes our checks ran (7 Oct 12:20–13:10; 7 Oct 19:30–20:00; 7 Oct 21:45–22:15; 8 Oct 09:20–09:45).
  • A child on the page is a new record, so a child on both routes is counted twice and some children called new since 7 October had played on the chat before (see “New and returning”).
  • The children are self-selected. They are the ones whose families opened a link. Nobody was watching them answer.
  • Every name on this page is invented. The screens come from our test copy of the service; no child’s or teacher’s name, class or number appears here.
