Children reading

Three ways we hear children read. The scores beside each recording are the AI's and a person's, so the gap between them is in plain view.

The numbers above come from the Rawalpindi trial: 2,233 children, 28 enumerators, 99 schools, 207 whole-session recordings and 1,462 re-listens by reviewers. The AI was evaluated on the 187 recordings that join to both. On timed reading, two trained people agree only about half the time, so that, not 100%, is the bar an AI has to clear.

After the lesson: the coach and the teacher

After an observation the coach and the teacher talk. We recorded those conversations and studied 399 of them: they are warm and safe (92–98%), the teacher rarely tries the new move out loud before the coach leaves (3% in Islamabad, 0% elsewhere), and the coached skill moved by +0.06 of a level against matched controls (95% interval +0.02 to +0.10).

How we chose these recordings

  • Real children only. We excluded test accounts, the team's own phones, test children, and every recording where an adult was reading. Each child clip passed a pitch check (a child's voice sits above about 260 Hz) and an AI listen confirming a child under about 12, the language, and that no one's name is spoken.
  • No names. No child, teacher, coach or school is named in any text here. Debrief text is redacted; coaches are numbered.
  • Sealed. Anything with a child's voice is encrypted on this page and opens only with the password; the debrief clips were already published in our study.
  • Short. Clips are 15 to 45 seconds, cut at a natural break, loudness-matched.
