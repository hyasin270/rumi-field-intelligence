The teacher.

Most children in the world go to a public school. Their teacher was trained once, when hired, and then put in front of 50 or 60 children with a textbook and a piece of chalk. For the next twenty years nobody asks them how it is going.

Coaching is different. A coach sits at the back of the class, hears the whole lesson, and afterwards talks it through with the teacher: what went well, and one thing to try next time. A session is one recorded lesson and the advice that comes back.

What changed.

In the last few years four kinds of software became cheap and good within a couple of years of each other.

Listening

software that turns speech into text.

Thinking

software that reads a transcript and says something sensible about it.

Seeing

software that turns a photo into text it can read.

Speaking

software that turns text into speech in the teacher’s own language.

So a lesson can be recorded, written down, marked and answered with a voice note. Nobody travels anywhere.

Rumi the coach: for teachers

Who sees what.

This is the first thing every government asks, since governments run the schools we work in.

The teacher,
and whoever coaches them

get the coaching report and the student scores.

The government

sees the trend across its schools.

Never

a teacher’s individual score.

We did not start with this rule, we learnt it. A government asked for every teacher’s score, we said yes, and two days later the whole district had stopped recording. Teachers will not record for someone who can punish them. We had to pick a side, and we picked the teacher.

Rumi the coach: for teachers

Once a week.

How often should a teacher use this? We went back over 28,403 recorded lessons from 3,432 teachers in Islamabad.

1

Once a week is what we ask now.

2 to 3

Teachers who recorded two or three times a week improved their checklist scores no more than the ones who recorded once.

×2

A teacher who had Rumi and a human coach improved about twice as much as a teacher with either alone. So we still work with human coaches, and the data tells them which schools to visit first.

July to October 2026. Published on hellorumi.ai in November 2026, with the finding that about one teacher in four scored worse after their first report.

Rumi the builder: for partners

Rumi the builder, for partners.

A partner is an organisation or a government that runs Rumi with its own teachers, in its own schools, under whatever name it likes. Running it for thousands of teachers is a lot of office work.

Lesson plans

Every grade and every subject needs a lesson for every teaching day, written from that region’s own textbooks: about 2,000 lessons for one primary curriculum.

Monitoring

Somebody has to see what happened yesterday across hundreds of schools, instead of in a quarterly report after the quarter is over.

Building and customising

Teachers and field staff write in with questions, bugs and requests for changes, and somebody has to answer them.

The builder does this office work: a staff of helpers, in the world of AI called agents. The people in the partner’s office set the direction and check each stage.

Rumi the builder: for partners

Given away.

The code, the written instructions that make the software run, has been on the internet since January 2026 under a licence that lets anyone copy it, change it, rename it and run it without asking us or telling us. That is what open source means.

In Islamabad the government pays us to run it, and the WhatsApp bot carries the institute’s own name, NIETE Teaching Assistant (the National Institute of Excellence in Teacher Education). In Rawalpindi, the district next door, the government’s own officers are the human coaches.

A developer in Niger found it, tested it in French and planned a small first trial with five teachers before anyone at Taleemabad had spoken to them. They taught us that our French was poor.

Organisations elsewhere, in India among them, have gone into the code and are making their own copy of it. A partner who takes it does not have to tell us.
